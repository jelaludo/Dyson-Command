    01 / QUICK START

    Your first five moves

    1. Choose Frontier campaign on the Command Map. Eos is yours: it is already terraformed and has 18 ships.
    2. Click Iona. In its action wheel, set the fleet slider to 9 and choose Launch fleet. Eos is the starting source.
    3. Select Wait one turn. The fleet arrives on turn 3. Nine ships beat Iona’s four defenders; five survive on your new foothold.
    4. Click your new Iona foothold and choose Terraform. It costs 40 stored power, takes one action, and turns Iona into a productive world with a receiver.
    5. Open Iona’s action wheel and choose Aim SEED C. You can also expand Collector alignment · advanced and select C twice to cycle Vesper → Kora → Iona. Its beam now contributes 22 output.

    Click a planet to open its action wheel. Choose Use as source on one of your worlds, then click the destination and choose Launch fleet.

    02 / DEFINITIONS

    What is what?

    Planet
    A node on the simplified star map. A number beneath it is the ships stationed there. Green means yours, blue means neutral, red means rival. A dashed green outline means a secured foothold that is not yet terraformed.
    Foothold
    A world you captured with a fleet. It holds any surviving ships, but has no ship production or working receiver until you terraform it.
    Terraformed world
    A world that belongs to you and has completed the 40-power terraforming action. It builds ships each turn and has a receiver. This is a game state, not a physical climate simulation.
    Receiver
    The planet-side station that accepts a beam from a star-orbit collector. It exists automatically on each of your terraformed worlds. One receiver can use one beam at a time.
    Collector
    A star-orbit light harvester, shown in the Collector alignment panel. HEL-01 / A and B, and SEED-01 / C and D, are four gameplay nodes. Selecting one rotates its transmission target through its listed reachable worlds.
    Stellar output vs stored power
    Output is the sum of currently linked collector yields. It can change immediately when you rotate a collector. Stored power is spendable energy: it increases by the current output when a turn advances, up to 240. Terraforming and laser pulses spend stored power.

    03 / FRONTIER CAMPAIGN

    Fleets, combat and turns

    • Click one of your worlds and choose Use as source. Click a different world, adjust Ships to send in its action wheel, then choose Launch fleet. The ships leave the source immediately.
    • Travel takes one to three abstract turns according to map distance. Fleets in transit appear as small markers and in the flight readout. Wait one turn moves them forward without ordering another action.
    • On arrival, an attack succeeds only if your arriving ships exceed the defenders. Survivors equal attackers minus defenders. A tie leaves the world uncaptured with zero defenders; send another ship. A fleet arriving at your own world reinforces it.
    • Every fleet launch, terraform action, laser pulse and wait advances one turn. Your terraformed worlds produce their listed ships each turn. Rival garrisons gain one defender on even-numbered turns; this prototype has no opponent fleet AI.
    • Current ship production: Eos 4, Iona 2, Talus 3, Kora 2 per turn after terraforming. Vesper and Nadir can also become productive if captured and terraformed.

    04 / LIGHT PUZZLE

    How do I rotate a collector?

    1. Expand Collector alignment · advanced on the Command Map. Each row is a button with a name, target planet and status.
    2. Select that row to rotate to its next reachable world. Repeated selections cycle through the list below and wrap back to the first. Rotation costs no power and does not advance a turn.
    3. On a terraformed world, its action wheel also offers direct aiming for compatible collectors. A beam is linked only when it points to one of your terraformed worlds and that receiver is free. Its yield is added to stellar output.
    4. Receiver offline means the target is rival, neutral or only a foothold. Receiver saturated means another collector already uses that planet’s one beam slot; the collector higher in the panel takes priority. Rotate to a different receiver.

    05 / OPTIONAL TACTIC

    What does the orbital laser do?

    In Frontier, select a red rival world as the destination, then use SOL laser. It costs 24 stored power, removes up to 7 defenders and advances one turn. It never captures the world by itself: send a fleet afterward. If the button is disabled, accumulate more power. The pulse is an abstract tactic inspired by the SOL asset family, not a physical weapon simulation.

    06 / OBJECTIVES

    How do I win?

    Frontier campaign

    Own and terraform 4 worlds and reach 75 stellar output at the same time. You may capture neutral worlds, fight rivals with fleets, or weaken rivals first with the laser. Capturing alone does not count: use Terraform on each foothold.

    Optics challenge

    The four receiver worlds start online. Fleet and laser controls are hidden. Rotate the collectors until output reaches 80. With the available collectors, this means routing all four beams to different live worlds for 82 output.

    Switch modes at the top of the Command Map. Restart scenario resets the current mode. Switching modes also starts a fresh scenario; it does not erase or modify any 3D model.

    DESIGN PROTOTYPE

    Three verbs, one growing system.

    Fleets take a foothold. Terraforming makes it productive and installs a receiver. Optical alignment raises stellar power; the SOL laser spends stored power to weaken a defended rival. Each choice changes the next turn.
